## 3. Stein Theory Setup (170 words)

- Define the main theory in course terms:
  - Regimes are not just organizations. For Stein, a regime exists when states move from independent decision-making to joint decision-making.
  - Anarchy remains; the regime does not create world government.
  - Regimes arise when independent action is costly and states need rules, expectations, or commitments.
- Distinguish Stein's two problems:
  - **Common aversions / coordination:** actors may not want the same ideal outcome, but they all want to avoid a bad one. Applicants and existing allies wanted to avoid Russian coercion and alliance fracture, but their rankings of costs and risks differed.
  - **Common interests / collaboration:** states prefer mutual cooperation, but each has incentives to shift costs, bargain for side payments, or under-contribute. Article 5 credibility, defense spending, risk-sharing, and Turkey's accession bargaining fit here.
- Citation placeholder: Stein, 1982, on coordination/collaboration and joint decision-making.
- Caution: do not reduce Stein to "institutions cause cooperation." The sharper claim is that regimes structure choices when unilateral action becomes worse.
