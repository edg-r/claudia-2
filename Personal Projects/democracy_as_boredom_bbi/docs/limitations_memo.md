# Limitations Memo

The Bureaucratic Boredom Index measures the rhetorical presence of procedural, institutional, administrative, and legalistic language relative to charismatic sovereignty language. It does not prove that a speech, leader, or country is democratic.

## Main Limits

- The pilot corpus is U.S.-only, so it cannot validate the index against cross-national democratic variation.
- Dictionary methods are transparent but blunt. Words such as `great`, `state`, or `people` can be context dependent.
- Crisis rhetoric can lower BBI even when the executive is operating within democratic institutions.
- Procedural language can appear in bureaucratic authoritarian contexts, so high BBI is not equivalent to liberal democracy.
- GDP begins in 1947, so GDP is missing for earlier speeches.
- The recession indicator uses monthly FRED USREC values and does not capture all political or security crises.
- The major-war indicator uses broad manually coded intervals and does not capture every use of force, emergency, or international crisis.
- Divided government uses Congress-level party control/trifecta status and does not model intra-Congress party switches in detail.
- The scraping pipeline depends on American Presidency Project page structure. The script includes a seed fallback but full analysis requires successful live retrieval.

## Appropriate Interpretation

Low BBI may indicate stronger use of charismatic, personalistic, or crisis-centered rhetoric, which may be associated with weaker rhetorical deference to institutional constraint. High BBI indicates more procedural and administrative language relative to charismatic sovereignty language.
