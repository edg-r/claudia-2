Course Information
This will be a practical, hands-on course whose purpose is the use of statistics to estimate
causal policy impacts using real-world data. The course is an introduction to the study of
causal inference for policy evaluation. In this class we will study the main identification
strategies that allow researchers and policy makers to draw credible causal conclusions from
data, including randomized experiments, difference-in-differences estimation, and regression
discontinuity designs.
The course emphasizes practical implementation alongside conceptual understanding. Students will learn when a specific method is appropriate, what assumptions are required, and
how to assess whether those assumptions are plausible in real-world settings. All methods
will be applied to policy-relevant questions, with a focus on techniques commonly employed
in program evaluation, development economics, health policy, and related fields.

Requirements and Policies
In the past, considerable emphasis was placed on at-home coding and analytical exercises.
However, AI tools now make completing these tasks relatively trivial. As a result, the emphasis in this course will be on conceptual understanding, analytical skills, and judgment.
The objective of the class is for students to develop both the interest and the ability to recognize challenges and opportunities related to causal identification and statistical inference,
as well as ways to work through them. The topics and requirements of the course reflect this
focus on developing conceptual and analytical skills.
You may use AI tools to help with coding tasks and to better understand the literature and
key topics. Assignment responses will be evaluated based on the quality of your reasoning
and interpretation, not on writing style. All written explanations must be composed by you.
Any use of AI tools to generate written responses for homework will be treated as academic
dishonesty and will be referred to the UCSD Office of Academic Integrity.

Plagiarism Policy
Students should work independently on all assignments, and homework will be submitted
through TurnItIn.com. Students may discuss the concepts behind homework questions with
each other, and may share snippets of code with each other for the purpose of improving
programming ability, but the writeup of the answers should be 100% individual. Any student
found plagiarizing on assignments will be given an F for the course and reported to the UCSD
Office of Academic Integrity.

Intended Learning Outcomes
This course develops expertise in the applied use of data to estimate policy impacts. Students
will learn key principles and statistical techniques across several types of analysis, including
cross-sectional methods (such as regression for causal inference and instrumental variables),
panel data approaches (including the intuition and use of two-way fixed effects, as well as
the basics of research design and why experiments help address causal inference challenges),
and time-series analysis (covering autocorrelation and basic forecasting techniques).
