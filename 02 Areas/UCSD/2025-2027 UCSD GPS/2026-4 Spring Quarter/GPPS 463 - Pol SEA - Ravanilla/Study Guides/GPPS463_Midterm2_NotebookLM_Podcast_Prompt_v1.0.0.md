Cover these Midterm 2 frameworks in this order if they are supported by the uploaded sources:

1. Growth accounting and Krugman's "Myth of the Asian Miracle"
   - Situation: rapid Asian Miracle growth in Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia, and other East/Southeast Asian economies.
   - Emphasize input mobilization versus total factor productivity, Cobb-Douglas/growth accounting logic, diminishing returns, savings, labor, education, and "perspiration rather than inspiration."
   - Case application: use Singapore and Thailand to show why high growth does not automatically prove a permanent productivity miracle.

2. Thailand's market-oriented, entrepreneur-led miracle
   - Situation: Thailand grew rapidly without a classic Korea/Taiwan/Singapore-style developmental state.
   - Emphasize Chinese-Thai entrepreneurial networks, trade openness, macroeconomic orthodoxy, small government, factor accumulation, Bowring Treaty legacy, and uneven distribution.
   - Case application: Thailand as the "market-oriented but not laissez-faire" miracle case and as a bridge between growth and later financial vulnerability.

3. Singapore's state-created comparative advantage
   - Situation: Singapore's post-1965 development as a small, vulnerable, resource-poor city-state.
   - Emphasize how the PAP state used markets while shaping wages, labor discipline, forced savings, housing, infrastructure, FDI, EDB, CPF, HDB, NTUC/NWC, Jurong, phase strategy, and SIJORI.
   - Case application: Singapore as an active administrative-state model that complicates a pure Washington Consensus or free-market reading of the Asian Miracle.

4. Wade's "Wheels Within Wheels" critique of the Asian Financial Crisis
   - Situation: the 1997 Asian Financial Crisis as a regional and global financial event, not just a domestic crony-capitalism failure.
   - Emphasize hot money, open capital accounts, global liquidity, credit booms, asset inflation, capital reversal, international financial markets, core economies, and capital controls.
   - Case application: Thailand and the regional spread of the AFC, with Wade as the global/IPE counterpoint to domestic-institution explanations.

5. MacIntyre's veto-player model of crisis response
   - Situation: investors during crisis need governments to be both credible and decisive.
   - Emphasize veto players, veto points, credible commitment, decisiveness, policy rigidity, policy volatility, policy risk, and the U-shaped risk curve.
   - Case application: Thailand, the Philippines, Malaysia, and Indonesia if supported by the sources; especially the tradeoff between too many and too few veto players.

6. Hicken 2008 on crisis severity, reform momentum, and Thailand versus the Philippines
   - Situation: Thailand was hit harder by the AFC yet generated deeper reform momentum, while the Philippines was hit less severely but failed to resolve major vulnerabilities.
   - Emphasize crisis as catalyst, mild-shock trap, Ramos reforms, political malaise, debt, weak revenues, GOCCs, constitutional reform, and reform pressure.
   - Case application: Thailand versus the Philippines as the central Midterm 2 recovery contrast.

7. Hicken 2006 on party fabrication and the rise of Thai Rak Thai
   - Situation: the 1997 Thai constitution and Thai Rak Thai changed Thailand's fragmented party politics.
   - Emphasize electoral incentives, mixed-member system, national party list, 90-day membership rule, reduced fragmentation, prime-ministerial leverage, superintendent institutions, programmatic party-building, and Thaksinization.
   - Case application: Thai Rak Thai as a more decisive post-crisis actor, but also as a source of concentration and democratic strain.

8. Malesky, Abrami, and Zheng on inequality inside single-party regimes
   - Situation: Vietnam and China are both fast-growing single-party regimes, but Vietnam had lower inequality.
   - Emphasize authoritarian institutional variation, winning coalition, coalition breadth, Central Committee versus Politburo/PBSC, semicompetitive internal elections, equalizing transfers, public spending, and fiscal equalization.
   - Case application: Vietnam versus China, with the key point that regime type alone does not explain distributive outcomes.

9. Lecture framework: Vietnam "doing well" under communism
   - Situation: Vietnam combines rapid growth, lower inequality than China, regime durability, and some governance capacity under single-party rule.
   - Emphasize why usual explanations are insufficient: Kuznets, globalization, population size, ethnic fractionalization, and broad single-party labels.
   - Case application: Vietnam's broader Central Committee, provincial and mass-organization representation, party congress incentives, equalization transfers, poverty programs, education spending, and public-service delivery.

10. Midterm 2 master synthesis
   - Situation: similar regional and global pressures produced different outcomes across Thailand, Singapore, the Philippines, Vietnam, and China.
   - Emphasize the master causal pattern: external pressure or growth opportunity -> domestic institution or coalition -> policy choice -> economic or political outcome -> limits.
   - Compare frameworks across IPE, institutions, crisis/recovery, and contemporary political economy:
     - IPE: Wade explains global financial vulnerability and cross-border capital structures.
     - Institutions: MacIntyre explains how veto authority shapes credibility and decisiveness.
     - Crisis/recovery: Hicken explains how crisis severity can produce or fail to produce reform momentum.
     - Growth and development: Krugman gives the accounting baseline; Thailand and Singapore show different institutional paths to mobilizing inputs and building competitiveness.
     - Contemporary political economy: Malesky, Abrami, and Zheng explain distributive outcomes inside authoritarian institutions.
