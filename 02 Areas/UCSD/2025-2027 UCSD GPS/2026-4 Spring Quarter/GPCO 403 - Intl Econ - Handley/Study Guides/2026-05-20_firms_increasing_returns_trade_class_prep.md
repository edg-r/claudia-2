## What Today Is Probably Doing

This class is the bridge from country-level trade models to firm-level trade. Ricardian trade says countries trade because relative productivities differ. Heckscher-Ohlin says countries trade because factor endowments differ. Chapter 6 adds a third story: even similar countries trade because firms face scale economies, consumers value differentiated varieties, and only some firms are productive enough to sell abroad (Feenstra & Taylor, 2021).

The core exam-ready sentence: **trade can raise welfare through lower average costs, more varieties, and productivity reallocation, even when countries look similar.**

## Terms To Have Ready

- **Increasing returns to scale:** doubling inputs more than doubles output, so larger production runs can lower average cost.
- **Economies of scale:** cost savings from larger scale. In this unit, the important intuition is that a bigger market lets surviving firms spread fixed costs over more units.
- **Monopolistic competition:** many firms sell differentiated varieties; each has some market power, but entry and competition limit profits.
- **Product differentiation:** goods are similar but not identical. Consumers care about variety, so trade in "cars for cars" or "electronics for electronics" can still create gains.
- **Intra-industry trade:** two-way trade within the same broad sector. This is different from Heckscher-Ohlin's cross-sector exchange of, for example, capital-intensive goods for labor-intensive goods.
- **Firm heterogeneity:** firms differ in productivity. Exporting is selective because selling abroad usually requires fixed costs.
- **Fixed export cost:** up-front cost of foreign-market access, such as distribution, certification, marketing, product adaptation, or compliance.

## Mechanism Map

1. **Market expands:** Trade opens access to foreign buyers.
2. **Scale rises:** Firms that sell more can spread fixed costs over more output.
3. **Average cost falls:** Larger surviving firms may produce at lower average cost.
4. **Variety increases:** Consumers gain access to more differentiated products.
5. **Selection happens:** Low-productivity firms may exit; medium-productivity firms serve the home market; high-productivity firms export.
6. **Productivity reallocates:** Market share shifts toward more productive firms, raising average industry productivity.

## How To Distinguish This From Earlier Models

**Ricardian question:** Which country has lower opportunity cost?  
**Heckscher-Ohlin question:** Which country is abundant in the factor used intensively by the export good?  
**Increasing returns / firms question:** What happens when market size, scale economies, differentiated varieties, and firm productivity differences matter?

If Handley asks why two rich countries trade similar goods, do not force a factor-endowment answer. Say: similar countries may trade differentiated varieties within the same industry because consumers like variety and firms use larger markets to exploit scale economies.

## Listen For These Lecture Moves

- Whether the model assumes **identical firms** under monopolistic competition or **heterogeneous firms** with exporter selection.
- Whether welfare gains come mostly from **lower prices**, **more varieties**, or **productivity reallocation**.
- Whether the example is about **intra-industry trade** among similar countries or firm selection after trade liberalization.
- Whether the distributional story is consumer gains versus workers/firms hurt by exit.
- Any graph showing **average cost falling with output**, firm demand under monopolistic competition, or an exporter productivity cutoff.

## Quick Self-Test Before Class

1. Why can Germany and Japan both import and export cars?
2. Why does a larger market reduce average cost for a firm with fixed costs?
3. Why do only some firms export?
4. How can trade raise average industry productivity without every firm becoming more productive?
5. What is the clearest difference between intra-industry trade and Heckscher-Ohlin trade?

**Answers:** 1. Differentiated varieties plus scale economies. 2. Fixed costs are spread over more units. 3. Exporting has fixed costs, so only sufficiently productive firms can cover them. 4. Market share shifts toward more productive firms and away from less productive firms. 5. Intra-industry trade is two-way trade in varieties within a sector; HO trade is cross-sector trade based on factor abundance.
