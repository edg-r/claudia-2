## Class Frame

Weeks 6 and 7 explained trade through **comparative advantage**: Ricardian technology differences and Heckscher-Ohlin factor endowments. Week 8 shifts the lens to why similar countries trade with each other and why trade happens at the **firm** level. The key move is from "countries trade because they are different" to "firms trade because scale, variety, and productivity differences make international markets valuable" (Feenstra & Taylor, 2021).

## Know These Terms Cold

- **Increasing returns to scale:** Output rises more than proportionally when inputs rise. Bigger production runs lower average cost.
- **Economies of scale:** Cost advantage from producing at larger scale. Internal economies happen within a firm; external economies happen when the whole industry or region grows.
- **Monopolistic competition:** Many firms sell differentiated products. Each firm has some market power, but entry and competition limit profits.
- **Product differentiation:** Firms sell varieties that are not perfect substitutes. Trade can raise welfare by giving consumers more varieties.
- **Intra-industry trade:** Countries both import and export varieties within the same industry, such as cars for cars or electronics for electronics.
- **Firm heterogeneity:** Firms differ in productivity. More productive firms are more likely to export because they can cover the fixed costs of entering foreign markets.
- **Fixed export cost:** Up-front cost of selling abroad, such as distribution, regulatory compliance, marketing, or adapting products to foreign buyers.

## Mechanisms To Be Ready For

1. **Scale mechanism:** Opening to trade expands the market. Firms that survive can sell more, spread fixed costs over more units, and lower average cost.
2. **Variety mechanism:** Consumers gain access to more product varieties. Welfare rises even if countries have similar technologies and factor endowments.
3. **Selection mechanism:** Exporting requires fixed costs. Low-productivity firms may exit, medium-productivity firms may serve only the home market, and high-productivity firms export.
4. **Reallocation mechanism:** Trade shifts market share toward more productive firms. Average industry productivity rises even without every firm improving internally.
5. **Distribution mechanism:** Gains are not evenly spread. Consumers may gain from lower prices and variety, while workers in exiting firms can lose.

## How This Extends Ricardian And Heckscher-Ohlin

- **Ricardian:** Trade comes from productivity differences across countries and goods. Exam move: compare opportunity costs, not absolute advantage.
- **Heckscher-Ohlin:** Trade comes from factor endowment differences. Exam move: abundant factor + intensive good = export prediction.
- **Increasing returns / firms:** Trade can arise even among similar countries because firms want larger markets, consumers value variety, and the most productive firms can profitably export.

## Mini Self-Test

1. Why can two capital-abundant rich countries trade cars with each other?
2. What happens to average cost when a firm with fixed costs sells to a larger market?
3. Why are high-productivity firms more likely to export than low-productivity firms?
4. How is intra-industry trade different from HO trade in capital-intensive goods for labor-intensive goods?
5. If the price of a capital-intensive export rises, which factor gains under Stolper-Samuelson?

## Answer Key

1. They trade differentiated varieties, and larger markets let firms exploit scale economies.
2. Average cost falls because fixed costs are spread over more units.
3. Exporting has fixed costs; high-productivity firms can sell at low enough cost to cover them.
4. Intra-industry trade is two-way trade in similar varieties within the same sector; HO trade is based on factor-endowment differences across sectors.
5. Capital gains in real terms; labor loses in real terms.
